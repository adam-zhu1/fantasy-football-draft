How to use this on draft day

  1. Set your slot (top right) once ESPN shows the draft order.
  2. Keep ESPN's draft room open next to this window. Each time anyone picks, type the name in box 1 and press Enter. If it was your pick, press Shift+Enter instead (or click the blue button).
  3. When the banner says YOUR PICK, look at the Recommendation. Take that player in ESPN, then mark him as yours here.
  4. Wrong click? Cmd+Z or the Undo button removes the last pick. Clicking a pick in the log flips it between mine and theirs.
  5. The draft is saved to data/draft_state.json after every pick. If the page or server dies, restart it and everything is still here.

The rules the board enforces (from your plan)

  • Kicker and defense only in the last two rounds. They score about the same as each other; drafting one early wastes a pick that could be injury insurance.
  • Exactly one QB and one TE. You only start one of each, and the bench versions score zero for you.
  • Everything else is RB or WR, until you have at least 5 RB and 6 WR. Those are the positions that get hurt. Depth means an injury costs you a downgrade instead of an empty slot.
  • Never reach across a tier break. Players inside a tier are near-equal; the drop between tiers is real.

Glossary

  • PPR: your league gives 1 point per catch, on top of yards and touchdowns.
  • ADP (average draft position): the pick number where a player usually gets taken in other people's drafts. Used to guess whether he'll still be there at your next turn.
  • Value (VBD, value over replacement): projected points minus the points of the last starter-quality player at that position, after discounting for missed games and weekly volatility. It's what makes a RB and a QB comparable.
  • Fit: Value plus bonuses/penalties for what your roster needs (open starting slot, too many from one NFL team, bye-week pile-up).
  • Lasts?: chance the player is still available at your following pick. Under 50% means "now or never".
  • Tier: a group of players with similar value. The dashed lines on the board.
  • Bye: the week that player's NFL team doesn't play. He scores 0 that week. Avoid piling 4+ starters on the same bye.
  • FLEX: a starting slot that can hold a RB, WR, or TE.
  • Snake draft: pick order reverses each round (1→12, 12→1, …).
